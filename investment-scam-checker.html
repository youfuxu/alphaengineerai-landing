Investment safety guide

Investment scam checker: pressure is not a return.

A message, group chat, relationship, celebrity video, or polished trading app can make an investment look credible. Slow down and verify the person, firm, offering, and payment route before sending money.

This is a fraud-safety guide, not investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Warning signs before you deposit

Guaranteed profit or “no risk”

High returns with little or no risk, guaranteed income, or a promise to beat the market are classic fraud signals.

Social-media or group-chat pressure

A stranger, influencer, fake expert, or group admin says you must act now, keep the opportunity secret, or follow a private link.

Fake balance or withdrawal fee

A platform shows profits but asks for more money, taxes, fees, or a minimum balance before you can withdraw.

Crypto, gift card, or personal-wallet payment

You are told to send funds to an individual, unfamiliar wallet, overseas account, or payment route unrelated to the named investment.

Check the opportunity independently

Pause the deposit

Do not send money because a countdown, limited slot, relationship, celebrity endorsement, or apparent profit is creating urgency.

Verify the person and firm

Use the relevant official regulator’s search tool to check registration or licensing where applicable. Do not rely on the promoter’s screenshot or link.

Understand where funds go

Ask how the investment works, who holds the money, how withdrawals work, and what risks apply. If the answer is vague or changes under pressure, stop.

Never pay to unlock or recover

Do not send a second deposit to release a fake profit or recover a first loss. Contact your bank, card issuer, or payment provider through its official app or website.

Official investment-fraud resources

These official resources explain common patterns and reporting routes. Open the official domain yourself rather than using a promoter’s link.

Investor.gov: Common scams

Review social-media groups, impersonation, emerging technology, crypto payments, and fake withdrawal fees.

SEC: Social-media stock scams

Check the current warning about group chats, fake professionals, and promises of high returns with little risk.

FTC: Cryptocurrency scams

Learn why guaranteed returns, fake testimonials, and requests to pay in crypto are warning signs.

Report securities fraud to the SEC

For U.S.-connected securities fraud, use the SEC’s official complaint route and also notify your payment provider.

Questions people ask before investing

Are guaranteed returns safe?

No. A guarantee of high returns with little or no risk is a major warning sign.

Can I trust a group-chat tip?

Not by itself. Verify the person, firm, and offering through an official regulator and independent sources.

Why is there a withdrawal fee?

A demand for more money to release a displayed balance is a common fake-platform pattern. Do not pay to unlock it.

What if I already paid?

Contact the real payment provider immediately, secure accounts, preserve evidence, and report. Ignore recovery-fee offers.
