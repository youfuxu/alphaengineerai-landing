Five warning signs to check first

Pressure and urgency

The message says you must act now, lose access, pay a fee, or keep the conversation secret.

Codes, passwords, or payment

A real service should not need you to hand over a login password or one-time verification code in a message reply.

An unexpected link

The link leads to a login, delivery-fee, investment, or payment page you were not expecting. Do not open it to investigate.

Impersonation

A sender claims to be a bank, delivery company, employer, friend, celebrity, or support agent but the request feels unusual.

A request to bypass verification

The person tells you not to use the official app, contact a known number, or tell someone you trust.

A safer first check

Stop the requested action

Do not click, pay, reply, download an attachment, or share a code while you are uncertain.

Remove sensitive details

If you use a checker, redact names, phone numbers, addresses, account numbers, passwords, codes, and private screenshots.

Verify independently

Open the official app or type the official website yourself. Use a known phone number, not the contact details in the message.

Report through the right channel

After the first check, use the official reporting route for your country or service.

Privacy rule: a suspicious message can be useful as a pattern example, but it should never require you to expose secrets.

If you already clicked or paid

You can still reduce harm. Stop contact, change exposed passwords from a trusted device, call your bank or provider through an official channel, preserve evidence, and report the incident.

Common questions

Can a checker prove a message is safe?

No. It can surface patterns, but you must verify through an official channel before acting.

Should I reply to ask?

Usually no. Do not use the suspicious message's reply path, link, or phone number.

What if the sender looks familiar?

Use a separate known channel. Accounts and caller IDs can be copied or compromised.

What should I never share?

Never share passwords, one-time codes, payment details, identity numbers, or private screenshots.

Uncertainty is a reason to slow down.
