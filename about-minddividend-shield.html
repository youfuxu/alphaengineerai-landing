About the public project

Clarity before action. Safety before sharing.

MindDividend Shield is a free global first-check project for people facing suspicious texts, emails, social messages, payment requests, account alerts, QR codes, and online pressure.

The goal is practical: help someone pause, remove sensitive information, recognize pressure, and reach a trusted official channel before a decision becomes harder to reverse.

What the project does

Starts with observable signals

Guides explain urgency, secrecy, impersonation, payment pressure, unusual links, emotional manipulation, and requests for access or codes without claiming certainty from limited context.

Points back to known channels

When money, accounts, devices, or identity are involved, the safest next step is usually the bank, platform, provider, or authority reached through a website, app, or number you find independently.

Works across languages and situations

The public directory connects first checks, common-scam guides, recovery checklists, and reporting routes across thirteen languages and multiple high-risk patterns.

Stays honest about limits

A first-pass signal cannot prove a message, website, person, payment destination, or investment is genuine. Uncertainty is a reason to slow down and verify.

Our safety and privacy rules

Never ask for secrets

The website tells people not to paste passwords, verification codes, full payment details, identity documents, wallet seed phrases, or private conversations.

Use redacted examples

Replace sensitive details with labels such as [NAME], [BANK], [LINK], [PHONE], or [CODE] before using a checker or sharing a public guide.

Do not impersonate authorities

MindDividend Shield does not represent a bank, government, social platform, police service, recovery firm, or professional adviser.

Measure without message text

The site’s analytics is configured for aggregate page and engagement measurement and does not send user message text, form input, passwords, codes, payment details, or other user-supplied content.

Before you check anything: if a message involves immediate danger, an active account takeover, or a payment in progress, contact the relevant emergency, bank, platform, or provider channel directly instead of relying on a website first.

How guides are built and updated

Pattern first

Each guide starts with a real-world pattern such as a fake bank alert, romance pressure, a QR code, a recovery agent, a delivery fee, or a job task request.

Official resources

Where appropriate, pages link to public prevention and reporting guidance from official consumer-protection, cybercrime, platform, or regional sources. Links are meant to be opened independently.

Machine-readable discovery

The public project maintains an RSS feed, sitemap index, robots file, and concise machine-readable guide map so people and tools can find the right starting point.

Public change history

Content and implementation are visible in the public GitHub project, with releases and discussions for new safety patterns.

Public reference

The resource is listed under Educational Resources in the community-maintained Awesome Consumer Protection directory. This listing is not an official endorsement or a safety guarantee.
