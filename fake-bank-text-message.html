Bank alert safety guide

Fake bank text message? Pause before you sign in.

A text that says your account is locked, a payment is pending, or fraud was detected can feel urgent. The safest response is to verify the alert outside the message.

Four warning signs

It creates a deadline

“Confirm in 10 minutes” or “your account will close today” is pressure, not proof.

It asks for a login or code

Never share a password or one-time security code because a text tells you to.

The link is unfamiliar

Look for misspellings, extra words, shortened links, or a domain that is not the bank’s known website.

The transaction is unexpected

If you do not recognize the payment, open the official app yourself instead of using the text’s link.

How to verify the alert

Do not use the message

Close it. Do not click, reply, call the number shown, or enter information on the linked page.

Open a known route

Type the bank’s website yourself, use the official app, or call the number printed on your card or statement.

Ask what happened

The official channel can confirm whether there was a real alert and tell you what action is required.
