The five-step reminder

1. Pause

Urgency, threats, countdowns, and emotional pressure are reasons to slow down, not reasons to act faster.

2. Do not use the message to verify itself

Do not call the supplied number, open the supplied link, or reply to ask whether the message is real.

3. Verify independently

Open the official app or type the known website yourself. Use a phone number, statement, or contact route you already trust.

4. Protect codes and payments

Never share a verification code, password, full payment detail, wallet key, or remote-access approval because someone asked unexpectedly.

5. Get the right help

If money, an account, identity, or a device may be at risk, contact the bank, provider, platform, or official reporting channel directly.

Copy this short safety reminder

Before you click or pay:
Pause. Do not use the message’s link or phone number to verify it. Contact the organization through an official route you find independently. Never share a password or verification code. If money or account access is at risk, contact the bank or provider now.

Share the reminder as text or send this page. It is safer to share a public guide than to forward a private message containing someone’s personal details.

Ways a community can use it

Family and friends

Send the reminder before a relative pays an unexpected fee, follows an account alert, or receives an urgent call from someone claiming to be a bank or family member.

Schools and educators

Use the five steps as a short digital-safety discussion, then point learners to a focused guide for a suspicious text, website, job offer, or AI voice request.

Creators and moderators

Link to a public first check instead of asking followers to post private screenshots. Encourage redaction and independent verification.

Support and community groups

Use the recovery and reporting guides when someone already clicked, paid, shared information, or needs an official reporting route.

Frequently asked questions

Who can use this toolkit?

Families, educators, community groups, creators, support workers, and anyone who wants a calm reminder before an online decision.

What can I safely paste into the checker?

Use a fictional or fully redacted example. Remove names, phone numbers, passwords, codes, payment details, identity documents, private addresses, and account information.

Does this prove a message is a scam?

No. It is a first-pass signal and education resource. Verify important claims through a known official route.
