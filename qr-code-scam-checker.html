QR code safety guide

QR code scam checker: inspect before you scan.

A QR code is a shortcut to a destination, not proof that the destination is safe. Scammers use codes in texts, emails, packages, parking signs, invoices, fake notices, and payment requests to create a fast path to a lookalike site or payment.

When the code is unexpected or the message creates urgency, pause and verify through a known official route.

Warning signs before scanning

Unexpected message or package

A text, email, letter, or parcel says you must scan to fix delivery, confirm an account, claim a prize, or see who sent something.

Urgent payment or threat

The code promises to avoid a fine, court action, account closure, utility shutoff, late fee, or lost refund if you act now.

Lookalike destination

The previewed URL has misspellings, extra words, strange domains, shortened links, or a destination unrelated to the organization named in the message.

Login, download, or crypto request

The page asks for a password, one-time code, card details, an app download, remote access, or cryptocurrency payment after the scan.

A safer scan decision

Do not scan the unexpected code

Do not use a QR code from a surprising text, email, package, sticker, invoice, or caller. Do not reply to ask whether it is real.

Open the official route yourself

Type the known website, use the official app, or find a trusted phone number on a bill or card. Check the notice there instead of using the QR code.

Inspect before any action

If you choose to scan a code you expected, read the destination before opening it. Stop if the domain, request, payment recipient, or login context does not match.

If you already scanned it

Close the page and do not download, sign in, or pay. If you entered information, change the affected password from the real site and contact the bank or provider through a known route.

Questions people ask before scanning

Is a QR code on a parking meter safe?

Do not assume it is. Inspect the physical label and destination, and use the parking operator’s official app or website when in doubt.

What if the code is from my delivery company?

Check the delivery through the carrier’s official app or website. Unexpected rescheduling fees and identity requests are warning signs.

Should I scan to see who sent a package?

No. Treat an unexpected package and its QR code as unverified. Do not enter credentials or payment details to identify a sender.

Can scanning alone steal my account?

The risk depends on what the destination asks you to do, but scanning can open a harmful page. Close it, avoid downloads and sign-ins, and act quickly if you shared information.
