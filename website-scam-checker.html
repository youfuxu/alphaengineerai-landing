What to check first

No single signal proves that a website is legitimate. Several pressure or identity mismatches together are a reason to stop.

Domain and destination

Look at the actual domain, not only the logo or visible link text. Misspellings, extra words, strange subdomains, shortened links, and lookalike characters deserve independent verification.

Pressure to act

Countdowns, “account closed” warnings, limited-time prices, forced chat, and urgent payment requests are designed to remove your time to verify.

Payment and login

Never enter a password, verification code, bank login, card number, or remote-access approval just because a page asks for it. Open the known official app or site yourself.

Company identity

Compare the company name, address, support route, return policy, and contact details with independent sources. A contact page alone is not proof.

Offer and pricing

Unusually low prices, guaranteed returns, crypto-only payments, gift cards, overpayment stories, or a request to move off-platform are strong warning signs.

Independent reputation

Search for the domain and company name separately. Read criticism carefully and remember that reviews, badges, and social profiles can be copied or manipulated.

A safer five-minute website check

1. Do not open it from the message

If the address arrived by text, email, DM, or an unexpected ad, do not use that link. Find the service through a bookmark, the official app, or a domain you already know.

2. Check the URL independently

Use Google Safe Browsing site status as one signal. A clean result is not a guarantee, and a warning means you should not proceed.

3. Verify the request outside the site

Contact the bank, store, platform, employer, or person through information you found independently. Never use the phone number or support link supplied by the suspicious page.

4. Check the message too

If a suspicious text or email led you there, remove private data and use the global message checker guide for a first-pass review.

5. Stop before the transaction

Do not test a site by making a small payment or creating an account. If the identity, request, or payment route is still uncertain, treat that uncertainty as a stop signal.

Safe input rule: never paste passwords, payment details, verification codes, identity documents, private addresses, or session links into a public checker.

What a website checker cannot tell you

A clean result is not a guarantee

New scam domains may not be listed yet, and legitimate sites can be compromised later. Re-check important claims through the official organization.

HTTPS is not a trust badge

Encryption protects the connection; it does not prove the operator is honest, the store will deliver, or the payment request is legitimate.

Reviews can be manufactured

Copied testimonials, fake badges, followers, and sponsored search results can make a site look established. Look for independent evidence and a known contact route.

For phishing warning signs and account protection, see the FTC phishing guidance.

If you already clicked, logged in, or paid

Stop interacting with the suspicious site. From a trusted device, change exposed passwords through the official service, contact your bank or payment provider, preserve evidence, and use the official reporting route.
