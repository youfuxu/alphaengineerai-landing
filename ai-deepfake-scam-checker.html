AI impersonation safety guide

AI scam checker: do not trust the voice alone.

A cloned voice, realistic video, or familiar profile can make a false request feel urgent and personal. Check the request through an independent channel before you pay, share a code, or reveal information.

Warning signs in a voice or video request

Urgency and secrecy

The caller says you are the only person who can help, demands immediate action, or tells you not to check with anyone else.

Unusual payment route

Gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, cash, or a new account are requested because the payment is hard to reverse.

Identity as the “proof”

A familiar face, voice, title, caller ID, verified-looking profile, or live video is treated as the reason you should skip normal verification.

Process bypass

A manager, supplier, family member, or official supposedly asks you to ignore the normal approval, callback, or account-security process.

Verify before you respond

Pause the request

Do not click the supplied link, call the number shown, send a code, or move money while the request is still creating pressure.

Use a known route

Call the person on a number already saved in your contacts, open the organization’s official app yourself, or start a separate conversation.

Ask an independent question

Use a family code phrase or a detail that is not visible in public posts. For work payments, require the normal second approver.

Protect the evidence

Keep the username, number, link, payment instructions, and timestamps, but redact passwords, codes, identity documents, and private addresses before sharing.

Questions people ask about AI impersonation

Can a voice or video prove identity?

No. Treat it as one signal only. Verify through a known number, official app, or separate conversation.

What if the voice sounds exact?

End the pressured conversation and call back using contact information you already trust. Do not use the number or link supplied in the request.

Should I share a verification code?

No. A one-time code is for the account or service that issued it, not for proving who is speaking to you.

What if I already paid?

Contact the real payment provider immediately, secure accounts, preserve evidence, and report. Ignore anyone asking for an upfront recovery fee.
