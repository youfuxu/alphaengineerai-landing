Relationship safety guide

Romance scam checker: love should not require payment.

An online relationship can feel personal and convincing. Slow down when someone you have not met in person asks for money, gifts, cryptocurrency, private documents, intimate material, or secrecy.

This guide does not judge a relationship or identify a person. It helps you notice pressure patterns and choose a safer next step.

Warning signs before you send anything

Fast intimacy and exclusivity

Someone expresses intense feelings quickly, calls the relationship secret or exclusive, or tries to make you rely on them before trust has had time to develop.

They cannot meet or verify consistently

Stories, location, job, profile photos, or video-call explanations keep changing. A polished profile or a few convincing calls are not proof of identity.

Love becomes a payment request

Travel, medical bills, visas, customs, family emergencies, gifts, gift cards, wire transfers, crypto, or an investment opportunity all turn affection into a financial decision.

Secrecy, guilt, or threats

You are told not to ask friends, to keep the relationship private, or to pay to stop intimate images, an accusation, or an invented emergency from becoming public.

Check the relationship without escalating risk

Pause payment and private sharing

Do not send money, crypto, gift cards, account access, identity documents, or intimate material because someone says it proves love or prevents a loss.

Verify outside the conversation

Talk to someone you trust. Check profile photos and public details independently, and use the dating platform's own reporting and verification tools rather than a link supplied by the contact.

Keep your boundaries visible

A genuine relationship can tolerate time, questions, and a refusal to send money. Pressure, guilt, anger, or a new emergency after you say no is useful information.

If money or material was shared, act quickly

Stop sending more, contact the bank or payment provider through an official route, secure accounts, preserve evidence, and report the account. Ignore recovery offers that require an upfront fee.

Questions people ask before replying

Is a money request always proof?

It is a serious warning sign, especially when the person is someone you have not met in person and the request is urgent, secret, or difficult to reverse.

What about a crypto investment tip?

A private relationship is not an independent basis for investing. Do not transfer money because someone promises profit or says a relationship depends on it.

Should I pay to stop a threat?

No. Paying often creates another demand. Preserve evidence, report the account, secure your devices and accounts, and seek help through an official route.

What if I feel embarrassed?

Being targeted is not a personal failure. Tell one trusted person early; distance and support can reduce further pressure and help preserve options.
