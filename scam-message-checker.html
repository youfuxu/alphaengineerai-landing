A scam message checker for the moment you are not sure.

You do not need to become a cybersecurity expert to pause a suspicious message. Start with the signals that scammers repeatedly use across texts, email, social DMs, job offers, delivery notices, and payment requests.

Five signals worth checking first

One clue can be harmless. Several together deserve a pause before you click, pay, or reply.

1. Pressure

“Act now,” “your account will close,” and emergency language are designed to remove your time to verify.

2. Unexpected money

Be cautious when a message asks for gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, unusual fees, or a one-time code.

3. Identity mismatch

A familiar name, logo, or profile picture does not prove the sender is real. Check the actual address or account.

4. Suspicious link

Hover or long-press without opening when possible. Look for misspellings, extra words, strange domains, or shortened links.

5. Too-good-to-be-true story

Unexpected prizes, guaranteed returns, effortless jobs, and urgent investment opportunities deserve independent verification.

6. Secrecy

Requests to keep a transaction secret or avoid a colleague, family member, bank, or official support channel are a major warning.

How to use the checker safely

Remove sensitive details

Do not paste passwords, payment information, verification codes, private addresses, or identity documents.

Run a first-pass review

Use MindDividend Shield to organize the visible pressure, money, identity, and link signals.

Verify independently

Open the company’s known website or call the number on your card. Never use the contact details supplied by the suspicious message.

Remember: a checker is a second pair of eyes, not a guarantee. When money or account access is involved, official verification is the final step.
