Is this email phishing? Check before you click.

A convincing logo or familiar sender name is not proof. Review the identity, request, link, attachment, and pressure pattern before you sign in, pay an invoice, download a file, or reply.

Five phishing email signals

One clue can be harmless. Several together are a reason to stop and verify outside the email.

Sender or reply-to mismatch

The display name looks familiar, but the actual address, domain, or reply-to route contains a different organization, misspelling, or unexpected country domain.

Urgent login request

An account warning, security reset, invoice, or document asks you to sign in immediately through a link supplied in the email.

Unexpected attachment

A document, invoice, delivery file, or shared-drive notice asks you to open a file, enable macros, or install software before you can view it.

Payment or secrecy pressure

A request changes bank details, asks for gift cards or crypto, demands a transfer, or tells you not to confirm with a colleague or the official provider.

Link and language anomalies

Look for shortened links, extra subdomains, strange characters, generic greetings, unusual grammar, or a story that does not match your recent activity.

Too-good-to-be-true reward

Unexpected refunds, prizes, jobs, investment returns, or account upgrades are often used to make you click before you think.

Use the checker safely

Do not use the email’s link

Open the official app or type the known website yourself. Do not call the phone number or reply address supplied by the suspicious email.

Inspect the real destination

Hover without opening when possible. Compare the destination with the organization’s known domain, not just the visible link text.

Verify the request independently

Use a saved phone number, a known colleague, or the official support channel. For invoices or bank changes, confirm through a second route.

Redact before using a tool

Remove passwords, codes, payment details, personal addresses, private attachments, and unique links before using MindDividend Shield for a first-pass review.

Remember: a first-pass checker can organize visible warning signs, but it cannot guarantee that an email is safe. Verify important requests through the official source.
