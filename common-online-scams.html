Common online scams have different stories. The pressure pattern is familiar.

This practical guide helps you recognize the first warning signs across bank alerts, deliveries, jobs, investments, marketplaces, social accounts, and tech support.

Check a suspicious message

Nine common scam scenarios

The brand and story change by country. The request usually tries to make you act before you verify.

Fake bank alert

An urgent account warning asks you to sign in through a link, confirm a code, or move money “for safety.” See the bank-text guide.

Fake delivery notice

A parcel message claims an address problem or small redelivery fee and sends you to a lookalike payment page. See the delivery guide.

Online job offer

A recruiter promises easy remote income, then asks for money, crypto deposits, personal documents, or reshipping. Check job-scam signals.

Investment or crypto pitch

Guaranteed returns, secret groups, celebrity endorsements, and pressure to deposit more are classic risk signals.

Marketplace buyer or seller

A person pushes you off-platform, sends a fake payment receipt, overpays, or asks for a verification code before a sale. Check shopping-scam signals.

AI impersonation

A cloned voice, realistic video, or familiar profile creates urgent pressure to send money or bypass normal verification. Check AI impersonation signals.

Account impersonation

A friend, manager, creator, or support agent asks for a code, gift card, transfer, or confidential information.

Tech-support pop-up

A warning says your device is infected and gives a phone number or asks for remote access. Close it and use official support.

Prize or romance request

An unexpected prize, urgent travel problem, or emotional connection turns into a request for money, secrecy, or account access.

The universal first-check sequence

Pause the requested action

Do not click, pay, download, transfer, share a code, or give remote access while the story is unverified.

Separate the message from the claim

A logo, caller ID, profile name, or familiar tone can be copied. Judge the actual address, account, and request.

Verify outside the message

Type the official website yourself, open the official app, or contact a known number. Do not use the contact details supplied in the message.

Protect the account if you acted

Change exposed passwords from a trusted device, contact your bank or provider, report the message, and preserve evidence.

Safe input rule: if you use MindDividend Shield, provide a redacted example. Never paste passwords, payment details, verification codes, identity documents, or private addresses.

Common questions

What should I do first when a message demands payment or a verification code?

Pause before paying, clicking, or replying. Do not use the link or phone number in the message; verify through the official app, website, or a known contact route.

Can I paste a private message into MindDividend Shield?

Only use a redacted example. Remove passwords, payment details, verification codes, identity documents, addresses, and account information first.

What should I do if the first check is uncertain?

Treat uncertainty as a reason to slow down. Do not send money or information; verify independently through an official channel and report or block the message when appropriate.
