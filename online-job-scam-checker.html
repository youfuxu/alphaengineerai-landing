Remote work safety guide

Online job scam checker: check the offer, not just the salary.

Fake remote jobs often promise easy money, flexible hours, or a fast hiring decision. The danger usually appears in what the “employer” asks you to pay, move, receive, or reveal.

Red flags in a remote job offer

Upfront payment

Real employers do not require a fee to unlock training, equipment, applications, or your first paycheck.

Crypto or money movement

Be extremely cautious if the job asks you to buy crypto, receive transfers, forward money, or use your account as a payment bridge.

Reshipping packages

Receiving parcels at home and sending them elsewhere can expose you to fraud and identity risks.

Recruiter pressure

Urgent offers, interviews only through chat, and requests for identity documents before a verifiable company process deserve a pause.

Verify before you apply or accept

Find the employer independently

Type the company’s known domain yourself. Check whether the role exists on its official careers page, not only in a forwarded message.

Check the sender and process

Compare the recruiter’s email domain, interview method, job description, and payment request with the company’s real process.

Protect your identity

Do not send passwords, one-time codes, bank logins, or unnecessary identity documents to an unverified contact.

Questions people ask before accepting a remote job

Should I pay to get the job?

No. An upfront fee for training, equipment, applications, or unlocking a paycheck is a major warning sign. Verify the employer independently before paying.

How do I verify the recruiter?

Find the company’s website yourself, confirm the role on its official careers page, and contact the company through a known channel rather than replying to the recruiter’s link.

What patterns should I watch for?

Be cautious with crypto tasks, money transfers, reshipping packages, chat-only interviews, urgent pressure, or requests for passwords, codes, bank logins, or unnecessary ID.

What if I already shared information?

Stop contact, secure affected accounts through official apps, contact your bank or payment provider if needed, preserve evidence, and report the fraud through an official route.
