How to tell if a text message is a scam.

A suspicious text often looks ordinary: a delivery update, bank alert, job offer, account warning, or message from someone you know. The story matters less than the pressure and the action it demands.

Use the pause-and-verify checklist

Did you expect the message?

If you did not recently place an order, request support, apply for a job, or enter a promotion, treat the message as unverified.

What does it want you to do?

Click a link, download an app, transfer money, buy gift cards, share a code, or “confirm” your login? Any one of these is a reason to slow down.

Is it creating fear or excitement?

Threats of suspension and promises of instant rewards both push you toward an emotional decision instead of an independent check.

Does the sender match the request?

A bank should not ask for a password or full one-time code by text. A friend asking for money may have a compromised account. Verify through a known route.

Can you verify without using the text?

Close the message and type the official website yourself, use a saved number, or ask the person through another channel.

Common fake-text patterns

Delivery and toll alerts

Unexpected “redelivery fees,” unpaid tolls, or parcel links are common ways to collect card details.

Bank and account warnings

Scammers use urgent lockout language to make you sign in through a lookalike page or share a security code.

Job and task offers

Be cautious when a remote job asks you to pay upfront, move money, receive packages, or use crypto.

Friend or family emergencies

Contact the person directly using a number you already have. Do not rely on the text’s explanation or contact details.
